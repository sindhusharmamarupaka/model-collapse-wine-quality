## 1 · Business Understanding

Synthetic data is becoming unavoidable — real data is often expensive, restricted, or simply running out. But if a model is trained on synthetic data generated by a previous model, and that cycle repeats, something can quietly go wrong: **model collapse**, where a model gradually loses touch with the real-world patterns it was meant to learn.

This project builds a small, controlled version of that cycle and asks one sharp question:

> **If a model's accuracy stays exactly the same across five generations of recursive synthetic training, does that mean nothing was lost?**

## 2 · Data Understanding

- **Source:** UCI Red Wine Quality dataset (Cortez et al., 2009) — real, publicly available physicochemical wine measurements
- **Raw size:** 1,599 records → **240 exact duplicates removed** → 1,359 unique real records used
- **Features:** 11 physicochemical measurements (acidity, chlorides, sulphates, alcohol, etc.)
- **Target:** Quality score grouped into 3 bands — Low (3–4) · Medium (5–6) · High (7–8)
- **Split:** 80/20 → 1,087 training records and a 272-record real test set, **held out and untouched for the entire experiment**

<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sindhusharmamarupaka/model-collapse-wine-quality/main/chart_class_balance.png" width="500">

The class imbalance here matters later — the **Low** class has only 50 training samples, versus 890 for Medium.

## 3 · Method

| Step | What happened |
|---|---|
| Generator | Class-conditional **Gaussian Mixture Model** per quality band, component count chosen via BIC/AIC |
| Pipeline | Real data → **Gen 1** → Gen 2 → Gen 3 → Gen 4 → **Gen 5** — each generation trained *only* on the previous generation's synthetic output |
| Evaluation | Every generation's classifier tested against the **same untouched real test set** |
| Signals tracked | Accuracy · F1-score · feature-distribution drift · correlation drift · within-class diversity |

## 4 · What I Caught — Two Layers Deep

**Layer 1 — accuracy lied.**

<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sindhusharmamarupaka/model-collapse-wine-quality/main/chart_accuracy_across_generations.png" width="560">

Gen 5 landed at **80.5%**, statistically identical to Gen 0. Judged on accuracy alone, this experiment would have concluded nothing was wrong. But look under the hood:

<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sindhusharmamarupaka/model-collapse-wine-quality/main/chart_feature_distributions_gen0_vs_gen5.png" width="640">

- **Citric acid's** real-data spike at exactly 0.0 (bottom-left panel) visibly flattens by Gen 5 — from **8.2% → 3.9%** of records
- **Alcohol's** sharp real-data peak (top-left) smooths into a wider, blander synthetic curve

**Layer 2 — I caught my own inflated number.**

My first diversity analysis produced this:

<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sindhusharmamarupaka/model-collapse-wine-quality/main/chart_diversity_initial_uncorrected.png" width="500">

*An alarming, uniform ~18–20% drop across every class.* Before reporting it, I traced *why* — and found a large share of that number came from my own **data-validity clipping step**, which compresses synthetic values back into the real observed range. That's a preprocessing artifact, not model behavior.

After isolating and removing that effect, the honest, defensible picture was smaller and far more specific:

[![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sindhusharmamarupaka/model-collapse-wine-quality/main/model_collapse_diversity_chart.svg)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sindhusharmamarupaka/model-collapse-wine-quality/main/model_collapse_diversity_chart.svg)

**The "uniform collapse" wasn't real — my own preprocessing was hiding where the actual loss lived.**

## 5 · Correlation Drift, Ranked (Not Cherry-Picked)

<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sindhusharmamarupaka/model-collapse-wine-quality/main/chart_correlation_heatmaps_gen0_vs_gen5.png" width="700">

| Feature pair | Gen 0 | Gen 5 | Abs. change |
|---|---|---|---|
| chlorides – sulphates | 0.43 | 0.70 | **0.27** |
| citric acid – residual sugar | 0.13 | −0.08 | 0.21 |
| chlorides – total sulfur dioxide | 0.05 | 0.25 | 0.20 |
| sulphates – alcohol | 0.08 | −0.12 | 0.20 |
| chlorides – pH | −0.30 | −0.45 | 0.15 |

Publishing the full ranked table — not just the single biggest mover — makes the finding defensible: chlorides-related drift is a genuine pattern across multiple feature pairs, not one coincidence.

## 6 · Honest Conclusion

> Under this specific setup, recursive synthetic training was associated with a small but measurable decline in within-class diversity, concentrated in the smallest, most data-poor subgroup (Low-quality wines) — while accuracy and the larger, better-represented classes remained essentially unaffected.

This is a deliberately **narrower** claim than "model collapse is real." The notebook's final sections show the reasoning for why — including the direct check on whether clipping itself inflated the result.

## 7 · Why It Matters Beyond Wine

- The riskiest failure mode isn't a crash — it's a **quiet, accuracy-invisible drift**
- **Minority subgroups lose the most**, and standard monitoring (aggregate accuracy) won't catch it
- Preprocessing choices (like clipping) can distort your own measurement of the problem — **test your pipeline assumptions, not just your model**

## 8 · Skills Demonstrated

- Simulation design: class-conditional generative modeling (GMM) with BIC/AIC selection
- Multi-signal model evaluation beyond accuracy (distribution, correlation, diversity)
- Methodological self-correction — isolating a preprocessing artifact from a real effect
- Statistical rigor: ranked, non-cherry-picked evidence tables
- Clear separation of a defensible claim from an overreaching one

## 9 · Limitations

- Wine Quality is a relatively "easy," smooth dataset for a GMM — a messier dataset could collapse faster
- Only 5 generations tested — whether the Low-class loss compounds or plateaus is untested
- A simple generator (GMM) was used, not a deep generative model (VAE/GAN)
- Only the diversity metric was re-tested for clipping sensitivity; distribution/correlation drift were not
